# Doubles Rules

Welcome to the most debated aspect of Mexican Train.
The oldest rule sets c. 1994 do not address many of the conditions that can happen when following these special rules.
House rules should be understood before gameplay begins in a new house.
To wit here are some questions you should ask when playing in an unfamiliar House

## Can I go out by playing a Double?
<form>

### <input type="radio" name="go-out-unsatisfied" checked> Yes
Most rule sets say a Round ends when a players lays his last Tile.
Since the Turn is not over until the last Tile has been laid, the Turn is the last Turn of the Hand and Doubles Rules never had a chance to go into effect since the player emptied his Hand.
### <input type="radio" name="go-out-unsatisfied">No
Some anarchists want to destroy the fun of the game by re-prioritizing the rules.  The vast majority of rule sets mention emptying ones Hand as almost the first rule.
Few mention an exceptions to this rule.
Some militant players interpret [Must Doubles be Satisfied](#must-doubles-be-satisfied) as a higher priority for unknown reason and require the game to continue.  This opens an interesting situatation where the Boneyard may be empty so the player cannot draw.  A subsequent player may Satisfy the Double leaving the player with no Tiles, still having no Tiles.  So then did the player who emptied his Hand magically win the Round when Doubles Rules ended?  Since this player has no Tiles what happens when his Turn comes around again? 
</form>

## Must Doubles be Satisfied?
<form>

See [Can I go out by playing a Double?](#can-i-go-out-by-playing-a-double)

### <input type="radio" name="Exceptions" > Only by the player who laid the double

Since the Doubles Rules require the Turn to end by playing another Tile, if the player laying the double cannot make another play (after drawing if necessary) then the Turn ends.
The player laying the double must place a Marker on his Private Train since he could not

### <input type="radio" name="Exceptions" > No.  Satisfying is optional
You can ignore the rest of this page but you eliminate some of the fun of the game.  Doubles Rules effectively never take effect.


### <input type="radio" name="Exceptions" > Yes, always
See [Can I go out by playing a Double](#can-i-go-out-by-playing-a-double) for some troubles with that.

* If all Tiles of the Rank have been played then rules require each player to draw a Tile until the Boneyard is empty.
There is no winner for the Hand.
All players total their Pips for this Hand and update their scores.
Regular play resumes (or the game ends if this was the last Hand).

* A player with no Tiles is denied a legitimate win for emptying his Hand.

### <input type="radio" name="Exceptions" checked>Maybe
* When all Tiles of the rank have already been played there is no way to Satisfy the double in this Hand.
The Train is derailed and no longer usable in this Round.
If there are other unsatisfied Doubles remaining, evaulate them in turn if the House Rules require satisfying in a certain order.
Otherwise Doubles Rules ends and regular play resumes for this Hand.
The current player must prove that all Tiles of the Rank are already on the board.
A simple count is all that is needed.

</form>

## How many Doubles can be laid on a single Turn?
<form>

### <input type="radio" name="how-many" checked> 1

A player may only lay one double and then must immediately play another tile, drawing if necessary.
The player must place a Marker on his Personal Train if unable to make another play.
Satisfying the Double by other players depends on the other Doubles Rules in effect.

### <input type="radio" name="how-many"> 2
If there are multiple Public Trains where a Double may be played, the player may lay doubles in any order.
The player must end his Turn by playing a third Tile that is not a Double.
The third tile may be played on an Unsatisfied Double or any valid Public Train.

### <input type="radio" name="how-many" checked> As many as possible
A player may lay as many doubles that match a Public Train.
The player must end his Turn by going out (if that rule is allowed) or by laying a non-Double.
If a non-Double cannot be played even after drawing if necessary, the players Personal Train is marked and Doubles rules remain in effect.

</form>

## Is there any particular order to satisfy Doubles?

<form>

### <input type="radio" name="order-of-satisfying" checked> No

Doubles may be satisfied in any order.
If a player cannot satisfy any unsatisfied double, even after drawing, the player must place a Marker on his Personal Train, even if he has a valid play if Doubles Rules were not in effect.

### <input type="radio" name="order-of-satisfying"> In the order played (First In, First Out / FIFO)
Doubles must be satisfied in the order played.
If a player cannot satisfy the next sequential unsatisfied double, even after drawing, the player must place a Marker on his Personal Train, even if he has a valid play if Doubles Rules were not in effect.


### <input type="radio" name="order-of-satisfying"> In the reverse order played (Last In, First Out / LIFO)
Doubles must be satisfied in the reverse order played.
If a player cannot satisfy the next sequential unsatisfied double, even after drawing, the player must place a Marker on his Personal Train, even if he has a valid play if Doubles Rules were not in effect.

</form>

## When do Doubles Rules end?

When a player empties his hand by Satisfying a Double.
Or when there are no more Unsatisfied Doubles that still have unplayed Tiles of the same rank.

A player could theoretically play Doubles where he holds the last matching Rank Tile of each Double.
Since Doubles can be satisfied, even if only by the same layer, all other players would have to draw from the Boneyard and place Markers on their Personal Trains.  This player would effectively have a monopoly on play while all other players have to draw each turn.
This is a rare but valid way to control the end-game and guarantee a win for the player with all the right Tiles.

## What if a Double cannot be Satisfied?
See [Maybe](#maybe)

## What if the Boneyard is Empty and there are unsatisfied Doubles?
Any player who needs to draw to Satisfy a Double loses his turn and play passes to the left.

## What if regular play begins and one or more opening Lines end in a Double?

<form>

### <input type="radio" name="order-of-satisfying">This is not allowed

When laying out the starting Line a player may not end his train with a Double.
The player would have to wait until regular play commences and play the Double then.

### <input type="radio" name="order-of-satisfying" checked> Same as order to Satisfy rule

See [Is there any particular order to satisfy Doubles](#is-there-any-particular-order-to-satisfy-doubles)  if there is a specified order the **first** Double is the one played considering the order of regular play.
Since the opening lines are considered to be built simultaneously, they would be satisfied by the player closest to the **first** player (FIFO) or the player the farthest from the **first player** (LIFO).

See [Choosing The First Player](choosing-first-player.md) to see where to start.

The **first** player for this Round must satisfy the **first** Double or place a marker on his Personal Train if unable.  Play continues until all opening line Doubles are Satisfied.

</form>

## What is a good rule set of Doubles

My house rules are
1. Emptying the hand means the Round ends.  Period.  Exclamation Point
1. When all Tiles of the Rank have been played the Derailed Train is ignored for the rest of the Round.
1. Any number of valid Doubles may be played on a single Turn.
1. A non-Double must end the Turn where Doubles were Opened.
1. The last Tile played does not have be on any of the Opened Doubles.
1. Doubles may be Satisfied in any Order.
1. Opening Lines are allowed to end in a Double.
1. Playing multiple Doubles would enable a player to bypass the **Uno rule** and go out since the round ended without the TUrn ever ending having only a single Tile.
(this shows the stupidity of the Tap or Uno rule that penalizes a player)
1. When a player cannot Satisfy a Double he must place a Marker on his Personal Train.
